Interested In Live Event Work?

Stagehand work is hands-on, physical, technical, and different from one call to the next. You might be unloading trucks, moving road cases, building staging, running cable, helping a department get set, or working a load-out after the show.

You do not need to know everything before you reach out. Some people come to us with experience in live events. Others are completely new to stagehand work and are not quite sure what to expect yet.

What Matters When You're New

Nobody expects perfection on a first call, but people remember whether you listened, took correction well, helped the crew, and gave an honest effort.

What matters is being honest about what you know. If you know how to do something, say so. If you don't, ask. We can teach somebody who's willing to learn. We can't work safely around somebody pretending to know something they don't.

Show up when you're supposed to. Pay attention. Stay engaged. Treat people with respect. Take safety seriously.

Do I Need Tools?

You don't need to go buy a pile of tools before filling out an interest form.

The form asks what tools you already have, including an option for "I don't have tools yet." That helps us understand where you're starting. It is not a reason to avoid reaching out.

Transportation

Reliable transportation matters because calls can happen at different locations and times.

The interest form asks about transportation, car access, and a driver's license, but car access and a driver's license are not hard requirements for submitting the form.

A Path Forward

People can enter the Local's work as extras, continue developing as apprentices, and work toward membership.

That process takes time, experience, training, and an understanding of the expectations that come with the work. The Training & Apprenticeship page explains more.
